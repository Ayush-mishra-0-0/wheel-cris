1. Multi-source data fusion: combine wheel measurements, maintenance history, route characteristics, environmental conditions, and operational telemetry rather than relying only on inspection records.
2. Spatio-temporal modeling: explicitly model how degradation depends on both time and the specific routes or track segments traveled.
3. Hybrid modeling: integrate domain knowledge (wheel wear indices, engineering constraints, or contact mechanics) with data-driven ML instead of using a pure black-box model.
4. Actionable outputs: predict Remaining Useful Life (RUL), maintenance priority, and recommended maintenance windows—not just wear values.
5. Explainability: provide feature attribution (e.g., SHAP) so maintenance engineers understand why a wheel is flagged.
6. Closed-loop learning: incorporate new maintenance outcomes back into the model to continuously improve predictions as more data becomes available. This aligns with recent research trends in continual learning for railway maintenance.
